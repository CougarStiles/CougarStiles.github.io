MARCO — Energy-Optimal Autonomous Ground EV

Graduate Research Assistant, Controls & Autonomy · Advised by Dr. Xingyong Song · Jan 2025 – Dec 2026

One predictive controller solving three problems at once: track the trajectory, avoid the obstacle, and minimize energy across a hybrid storage bus. Predictive is the word doing the work. Because it solves over a horizon, the power split is decided against the demand that is coming rather than the one that already hit, and the supercapacitor takes the transient before the battery ever sees it. It runs on the vehicle, and the loop is perception-bound rather than solver-bound.

RIPTIDE — Automated 3D-Scanning QC Cell

Mechatronics Engineer, Senior Capstone Team · Additive at Scale · Nov 2023 – Dec 2024

A UR5 carries both the 3D scanner and a claw on one end effector, so it never swaps tools between handling a part and measuring it. It picks up a frame, sets it on a rotating platform, and scans the part while the platform turns it. I designed the claw and that rotary stage. The cell replaced a manual inspection that ran fifty to sixty minutes with one that runs six to ten minutes depending on setup. Published at the 22nd International Conference on Ubiquitous Robots.

Desktop Board Repair Robot

Project Manager & Lead Engineer · BYRD Innovations · Nov 2022 – Aug 2023

I led the team that built a machine to repair computer mice: desolder, clean, and resolder any combination of the encoder wheel and the button array, in about five minutes. It is a precision manipulation problem with a thermal process running in the middle of it. I also supported the senior team's companion machine, which finds and replaces surface-mount chips and heatsinks on digital mining boards.

Repair cycle

  1. Setup. Operator checks the machine for obstructions or damage, loads the board into the centre fixture and a component cartridge into its holder, then plays the repair profile.
  2. Home and calibrate. The robot homes and calibrates itself and the activated-charcoal fume fan above it starts.
  3. Desolder. The top front and back faces carry a pair of chopstick-style flat-blade desoldering irons and a gripper. Each part removed drops down a discard chute.
  4. Drop down.
  5. Clean the board. The bottom front and back faces pair a hot air gun with a vacuum nozzle that hooks over the board, blowing from one face while drawing solder through the through-holes from the other into a cyclonic filter that cools and collects it.
  6. Rotate the board 90 degrees.
  7. Resolder. The left and right faces load new parts from the cartridge, then a single-point iron and solder extruder lay down solder pin by pin.
  8. Cool and rehome. The robot cools and returns home so the operator can safely remove the repaired board and the spent cartridge, and clear waste every few cycles.
